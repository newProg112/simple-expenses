Privacy Policy

How Simple Books handles personal information

This draft explains the current Simple Books implementation. It is not a final published notice or certified legal advice. Items marked for owner confirmation must be resolved before publication.

Draft date: 2 September 2026

1. Who operates Simple Books

Proposed operator: Adam Elvin trading as Simple Books. This identity is awaiting owner confirmation.

Privacy enquiries and requests can be sent to adam@simple-books.co.uk.

Publication blocker: confirm the operator identity, the appropriate business or correspondence address to publish, and whether any additional contact or registration details are legally required. No address or telephone number has been inferred from unrelated sources.

2. Whose information and which role

For account administration, service operation, support, security, analytics and Simple Books billing, the proposed operator decides why and how personal information is used and is expected to act as controller.

Customers may enter information about their own clients, customers, suppliers, workers or other people in invoices, bills, expenses, projects, notes and attachments. The customer decides why that information is recorded and must have an appropriate basis for using it. Depending on the circumstances, the customer may be the controller and Simple Books may process that information on the customer’s behalf.

Publication blocker: obtain legal review of the controller/processor roles and put suitable data-processing terms in place if Simple Books acts as a processor. This Privacy Policy alone is not intended to be those terms.

3. Information handled

Account and contact information

Name, business name, email address, account identifier, sign-in and account state, support correspondence, and profile or business details entered in the Account area. Email-and-password authentication is provided through Firebase Authentication. Simple Books does not place the password in its business records or local business-profile cache.

Business records and files

Information entered in clients or customers, invoices, bills, expenses, mileage, projects, budgets, banking records, journals and related notes or references. This may include names, contact details, addresses, transaction descriptions, dates and financial amounts relating to other people.

Supporting bill or expense documents and a company logo can be uploaded to user-specific Firebase Storage paths. Business records retain file references needed to display or download available attachments. A selected bank-statement CSV is read and mapped in the browser; the raw CSV is not uploaded by that workflow, but confirmed mapped transaction fields are stored.

Subscription and payment information

Stripe processes checkout and payment-card entry. Simple Books stores the Stripe customer and subscription references, subscription status, price and billing mode required to manage access. It may store card brand and the final four digits for display, but does not store the full card number in Simple Books business records.

Technical, usage and browser information

Firebase, Stripe, OpenAI, Sentry and content-delivery services may receive technical request information such as IP address, date and time, browser or device details, and requested URLs as part of providing their services. Simple Books also records limited operational, account-activity and usage information needed for security, support, allowances and subscription processing.

4. Why information is used and proposed lawful bases

Provide accounts and requested product functions
To authenticate users, store and retrieve their records, provide exports, operate optional AI features and administer subscriptions. The proposed basis is that this is necessary to take requested pre-contract steps or perform the service contract.
Security, reliability, support and misuse prevention
To enforce ownership controls, diagnose errors, respond to support requests and protect the service. The proposed basis is legitimate interests, subject to documenting the interests, necessity and balancing assessment.
Billing and business administration
To process and evidence subscriptions and payments. The proposed bases are contract and, where a specific record must be kept by law, legal obligation. The exact records and obligations must be confirmed.
Product analytics
To understand a limited set of events and improve the service. The proposed data-protection basis and any consent required for device storage or access must be confirmed before publication.
Optional choices
Where consent is the selected basis, it must be specific and freely given and can be withdrawn. The signup Privacy Policy link is a notice, not blanket consent to every use.

5. AI Assistant and document scanning

The optional AI features send requests to OpenAI through authenticated Firebase Functions.

  • AI Assistant: the user’s question and a limited, question-relevant summary are sent. Depending on the question, the summary can include customer, supplier, project or budget names, dates, counts and financial figures. The request-building code removes email-address patterns and web links from the generated summary, but a user could still type personal information directly into their question.
  • Document scanning: the selected supported image or PDF content and a short extraction instruction are sent so possible bill or expense fields can be returned for review. The record is not saved merely because it was scanned.

The current provider requests set store: false. This draft does not make an AI-training, deletion, location or supplier-retention guarantee; those matters require confirmation against the applicable supplier terms and account settings.

6. Browser storage, caching, analytics and logging

Simple Books uses browser local storage for some account and business-record caches, interface preferences and local reminders such as whether an export was downloaded. Session storage is used for limited session or demo safeguards. Anyone with access to the browser profile may be able to access information stored there, so shared devices should be used carefully.

The site includes web-app installation metadata, but the inspected code does not register a service worker or promise an offline copy. Browsers and content-delivery services may still cache public assets under ordinary web caching rules.

Firebase Analytics is configured on non-local pages for a restricted set of events: email signup or login method, coarse invoice-creation details, scan file type and plan, AI-question plan, and the start of a GBP 15 Pro checkout. The event policy does not send invoice values, names or free-text descriptions as custom event parameters. Firebase Analytics may also handle standard device, session and request information.

Sentry loads only on the approved production hosts for error monitoring. The integration removes user fields, request bodies, headers, cookies, query strings, extra fields and non-navigation breadcrumbs before sending an event, and removes query strings and fragments from URLs. Error messages and stack information can still contain technical context, so this is a minimisation control rather than a guarantee that every event is anonymous.

Publication blocker: determine and implement the required UK consent mechanism for Analytics and any non-essential browser storage or similar technologies. Analytics currently initializes without a visible consent choice.

7. Service providers and international transfers

The current implementation uses:

  • Google Firebase for hosting, authentication, database, file storage, Cloud Functions and Analytics;
  • Stripe for subscription checkout, payment processing and the billing portal;
  • OpenAI for the optional AI Assistant and document-scanning requests;
  • Sentry for production error monitoring; and
  • Google-hosted Firebase SDK files and jsDelivr-hosted frontend libraries on relevant pages.

Some providers may process information outside the UK. This draft deliberately does not state hosting regions, processing locations, contractual safeguards or adequacy arrangements because those facts have not been verified.

Publication blocker: confirm the contracting entities, processing locations, subprocessors and applicable UK international-transfer safeguards for each provider, then state them accurately.

8. Retention, export and deletion

Account and business information is kept while needed to provide the account and operate the service. Some information may need to be kept for security, dispute, billing or legal reasons, but no complete retention schedule has yet been approved.

Users can download supported JSON, workbook, report or Accountant Pack outputs. No export is a complete copy of every service record. Depending on the export, omissions include authentication data, billing state, administrative or operational records, attachments, logos, payment or settlement history and generated reports. Downloads are user-controlled copies, not an automatic backup or disaster-recovery guarantee.

The Account page provides an irreversible deletion process following recent reauthentication. The implemented workflow cancels a linked Simple Books subscription, removes user-uploaded files, removes Simple Books Firestore account data, and deletes the Firebase Authentication user. It also clears identified Simple Books browser caches in the completing browser. Provider-side transaction, security, backup or legal records may not be erased by that workflow.

Publication blocker: approve a retention schedule covering account data, billing and tax records, support messages, analytics, security logs, AI requests, backups and provider-held records, including what remains after account deletion.

9. Security and its limits

Simple Books uses authenticated Firebase access, owner-based Firestore rules, user-specific Storage paths, server-side billing controls and restricted production error monitoring. These measures reduce risk but no internet service, browser, provider or transmission method can be guaranteed completely secure or continuously available. Users are responsible for protecting their credentials, devices and exported files.

10. Individual rights and complaints

Depending on the circumstances and applicable law, individuals may have rights to be informed, obtain access, correct information, request erasure or restriction, object to processing, receive portable information, and withdraw consent where consent is relied on. A request can be sent to adam@simple-books.co.uk. Identity may need to be verified before a request is fulfilled.

If information was entered by a Simple Books customer about another person, that person may need to contact the customer as the relevant controller. Simple Books will assist where its legal role requires it.

Concerns should first be raised with Simple Books. Individuals can also complain to the UK Information Commissioner’s Office using the ICO complaint service.

11. Changes and contact

A final policy should state how material changes will be communicated and when each version takes effect. Questions about this draft can be sent to adam@simple-books.co.uk.
